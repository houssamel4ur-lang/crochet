Crochet Earbud Cord Wrap with a Buttonhole

By Houssam El Ouardy · Published November 17, 2026 · Updated November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Make a slim single-crochet earbud cord wrap with a reinforced buttonhole, a securely sewn button and a tidy way to bundle a cable.

A small crochet cord wrap keeps wired earbuds from becoming a knot in a pocket or bag. This design is a narrow, flexible strip with a button at one end and a reinforced buttonhole near the other. The cable is gathered into a loose loop and the wrap closes around it. The fabric should hold its shape but remain soft enough to sit comfortably around a cable.

Materials and sizing

Use smooth cotton or cotton-blend DK yarn, a 3 mm hook, one flat 10–12 mm sew-through button, tapestry needle and scissors. The sample is approximately 2 cm wide and 11–12 cm long before fastening. Exact size depends on yarn and tension. Choose a button with holes large enough for your needle and yarn. A flat button sits more comfortably in a pocket than a shank button. Avoid thick or fuzzy yarn because it makes the buttonhole bulky and can catch on the cable.

Abbreviations

Ch means chain; sc, single crochet; sl st, slip stitch; st(s), stitch(es); RS, right side; WS, wrong side. Use US crochet terms. The turning chain does not count as a stitch. Work the first sc in the first stitch at the base of the turning chain. Count every row before turning so the side edges remain straight.

Make the main strip

Ch 7.

  • Row 1: sc in the second chain from the hook and in each chain across (6). Ch 1 and turn.
  • Rows 2–25: sc in each of the 6 stitches (6). Ch 1 and turn after every row.

Shape the buttonhole end

The buttonhole is placed at the end opposite the starting tail. Work across the six stitches:

  • Row 26: sc in the first 2 stitches, ch 3, skip the next 2 stitches, sc in the final 2 stitches (4 sc and a 3-chain opening). Ch 1 and turn.
  • Row 27: sc in the first 2 stitches, work 3 sc into the chain-3 space, sc in the final 2 stitches (7 sc). Ch 1 and turn.
  • Row 28: sc in each stitch across (7). Fasten off and weave in the end.

The opening should fit the chosen button without stretching. If the button is smaller, use ch 2 and skip 2 stitches in Row 26; if larger, make ch 4 and skip 3 stitches, then check that the bar across the opening remains strong. Do not make a slit by cutting the crochet fabric.

Add a neat border

With the RS facing, join yarn at one short end. Work 1 sc into the end of each row along the first long side, 2 sc in the corner, 6 sc across the starting edge, 2 sc in the next corner, then 1 sc into each row end along the second long side. At the buttonhole end, work 1 sc in each stitch and 2 sc around the outer corners, taking care not to close the opening. Sl st to the first border stitch and fasten off.

The side edges contain 28 row ends, so aim for 28 sc on each long side. If the edge ripples, reduce an occasional stitch; if it draws inward, add a stitch where needed. Keep the buttonhole opening clear and check that the border does not cover its center.

Sew on the button

Lay the strip flat with the buttonhole at one end. Fold the opposite end over the buttonhole end to find where the button should sit. Mark the center of the strip and sew the button to the starting end using matching yarn or strong sewing thread. Pass through each button hole several times, keeping the button secure but leaving enough room for the crochet fabric to slide beneath it. Knot on the WS and weave the tail through several stitches.

Test the closure before using it. The button should pass through the opening with a little resistance, but the fabric should not distort. If the button is difficult to push through, widen the chain space by one chain in a replacement buttonhole row rather than forcing it.

Use the cord wrap

Unplug the earbuds before wrapping. Gather the cable into a loose oval, keeping the earbud ends and plug from bending sharply. Place the wrap around the middle of the bundle and pass the button through the buttonhole. The cable should remain loose enough that it is not pinched. Do not wind the cord tightly around the wrap or use the closure to compress the bundle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a different button size?

Yes. Adjust the chain-space width to the button and test the fit before fastening off.

Will this fit every earbud cable?

No. Cable thickness and bundle size vary. Measure your own loosely gathered cable and change the number of rows if needed.

Can I use acrylic yarn?

Yes, but cotton usually holds the buttonhole shape better and is less stretchy.

Can I attach it directly to my phone?

No. This design is a cable wrap only. Do not attach it to a phone port, charging connector or device opening.

How should I wash it?

Remove the button if practical and follow the yarn label. Dry the wrap completely before using it with electronics.

Final check

Confirm that the long edges lie flat, the buttonhole is open, and the button is sewn securely. Keep the wrap separate from plugged-in devices and never tighten it around a cable. Pattern note: This written pattern has not yet been physically crocheted and tested as a finished sample; check the dimensions and button fit on your own sample before regular use.
