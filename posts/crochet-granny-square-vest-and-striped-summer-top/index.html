Two Crochet Summer Tops to Make: Granny Square Vest and Striped Tee

A handmade summer wardrobe can move between two very different crochet moods: the graphic, patchwork look of a granny-square vest and the easy rhythm of a striped short-sleeve top. This guide brings both styles together, explaining how to plan each garment, choose yarn, adapt the fit and finish the edges neatly.

Yarn, tools and preparation

For either design, choose a smooth DK cotton or cotton blend. Cotton gives stitch definition and feels breathable, while a cotton-acrylic blend can add softness and drape. Check the yarn label for meterage and care instructions before buying; the amount depends on garment size, motif count, sleeve length and personal tension.

You will need a suitable crochet hook, tape measure, stitch markers, scissors, tapestry needle and a notebook for recording measurements. Make a swatch, wash or block it as you plan to care for the garment, then measure it when fully dry.

US crochet abbreviations

  • ch: chain
  • sl st: slip stitch
  • sc: single crochet
  • hdc: half double crochet
  • dc: double crochet
  • sp: space
  • sk: skip
  • rep: repeat
  • RS / WS: right side / wrong side

A granny cluster in this guide means 3 dc worked into the same chain space. A turning chain counts only when the instructions explicitly say it does.

Style one: floral granny-square vest

The first design uses individual floral-inspired squares arranged into front and back panels. The square motif makes it easy to adjust the width and length without changing the basic stitch pattern. The reference has a light, open look, so avoid dense yarn or a very tight hook.

Plan the fit before making motifs

Measure the fullest part of your bust and decide how much ease you want. For a relaxed vest, a finished circumference around 5–10 cm (2–4 in) larger than the body measurement is a useful starting point. Divide the desired circumference into front and back panel widths. Then make one test square and measure it after blocking.

Estimate the number of squares across by dividing the target panel width by the blocked square width. Round to a practical whole number and adjust the square size or border rather than forcing a partial motif at the side seam. Repeat the same calculation for the body length.

Make a simple floral granny square

Round 1: With the center color, make a magic ring. Ch 3 (counts as dc), work 2 dc into the ring, ch 2. Repeat 3 times, then work 3 dc and close with a sl st to the top of the beginning chain. Tighten the ring. You should have four 3-dc groups separated by four corner spaces.

Round 2: Join the petal color in any corner space. Ch 3, work 2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc in that same corner. In each remaining corner, work (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Join with a sl st.

Round 3: Join the background color in a corner. Work a corner group of (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in each corner. In each side space between corners, work 3 dc. Join and fasten off.

This creates a compact three-round motif. If your sample is too small, add another round using the same rule: a 3-dc group in each side space and a corner group in every corner. Keep all motifs the same size.

Assemble the vest panels

Arrange the squares on a flat surface before joining. Alternate the flower centers or use a deliberate color repeat, then photograph the layout so you can keep the order consistent. Join squares with a whip stitch through the back loops or with a slip-stitch join. A sewn join is usually softer; a slip-stitch join creates a visible ridge.

Make a front panel and a back panel to the same width. For a simple vest, leave a center opening between the two front sections. Join the shoulders, leaving enough room for the neck opening, then seam the side edges from the hem upward. Stop below the shoulders to create armholes. Try the vest on before finishing the neckline and armholes.

Finish the neckline, armholes and hem

Work one round of sc around each opening, placing stitches evenly along the square edges and seam joins. At corners, add a stitch only if the edge pulls inward; too many stitches can make it ruffle. For a clean hem, work one or two rounds of sc or hdc. Block the vest gently to even the motif edges.

Style two: relaxed striped summer top

The second reference is a lightweight tee with horizontal color bands, short sleeves and a softly scalloped lower edge. Its simplest construction is two matching body panels joined at the shoulders and sides, with sleeves worked into the armholes.

Choose stripe colors and gauge

Select two or three colors with similar yarn weight. Plan the stripe sequence before starting and write down the number of rows for each color so the front and back match. Carry yarn only if the carried strand remains hidden and does not pull the edge; otherwise cut and rejoin at each stripe.

Make a swatch in the stitch pattern you intend to use. A breathable fabric can be made with alternating rows of dc and chain spaces:

Foundation: Chain a multiple of 2 plus 1.

Row 1: Dc in the fourth chain from the hook (the skipped chains count as the first dc). Ch 1, sk 1 ch, dc in next ch. Repeat across.

Row 2: Ch 3, turn. Dc in each dc, working 1 dc into each ch-1 space. This creates a more stable mesh stripe.

For a denser tee, use plain dc rows instead. Check the swatch for transparency and stretch before deciding.

Measure and crochet the body

Measure your bust and desired top length. For a relaxed fit, add a modest amount of ease. Use your swatch to calculate the foundation chain needed for half the finished circumference. Work the front and back panels to matching dimensions, changing colors at the planned stripe intervals.

Keep the side edges straight by working the first and last stitches consistently. Count rows at each color change. Finish both panels at the same point in the stripe sequence so the stripes line up at the shoulders and side seams.

Shape the neckline and join the shoulders

Lay the panels together and mark the center neckline opening. Join the outer shoulder sections, leaving a comfortable opening for the head. A shallow boat neck is easiest with rectangular panels; a deeper front scoop requires leaving more stitches unjoined at the front center. Try the panels on before seaming.

Join the side seams from the hem upward, stopping at the armhole depth you want. Use markers to make both armholes equal.

Add short sleeves

Join yarn at the underarm and work a setup round of sc around the opening. Distribute stitches evenly along the vertical edges and across the shoulder join. Work a few rounds of dc or the mesh stripe pattern, checking sleeve length against your upper arm. Finish with one round of sc or a small scallop edge.

To make a scallop, repeat sl st in the next stitch, skip one stitch, work 5 dc in the next stitch, skip one stitch. Adjust the repeat at the end of the round so the edge lies flat. If it waves, reduce the number of stitches in each shell or use a smaller hook.

Fit, blocking and finishing checklist

  • Compare front and back widths before joining.
  • Match stripe rows at both side seams.
  • Check that shoulder joins do not sit too close to the neck.
  • Try the garment on before closing the side seams completely.
  • Weave in every tail securely on the wrong side.
  • Block motifs and panels to measured dimensions; do not stretch cotton lace aggressively.
  • Dry flat if the yarn stretches when wet.

Common problems and fixes

Granny squares curl: Check that the corner spaces have the correct chain count and that the hook is not too small. Block a sample square before making the full set.

Motifs do not match in size: Use the same yarn, hook and tension for every square. Measure after blocking, not immediately off the hook.

Striped panels look uneven: Record the row count for every stripe and use the same sequence on both panels.

The tee feels too heavy: Try a lighter cotton yarn or a more open stitch pattern. A dense fabric can feel warm even when the yarn is cotton.

The scalloped edge flares: Reduce stitches in each shell or add more spacing between shells.

Frequently asked questions

Which top is easier for a confident beginner?

The striped top is usually easier to plan because it uses two panels and repeated rows. The granny-square vest involves motif consistency and joining, but each square is a small, manageable unit.

Can I use acrylic yarn?

Yes, if the yarn is comfortable against the skin and has suitable drape. Make and wash a swatch first because some acrylic yarns relax or stretch differently from cotton.

Can I change the colors?

Absolutely. Keep the stripe sequence written down, and test granny-square color combinations with a few sample motifs before making the full set.

How do I know the garment will fit?

Use your own body measurements and blocked gauge. The reference pictures cannot provide reliable finished measurements or stitch counts.

Final note

Both styles reward careful measuring more than rushing. Make one complete motif or a generous swatch first, record your gauge, and use that sample to calculate the garment. The finished result should fit comfortably when worn, not merely resemble a picture on a screen.
