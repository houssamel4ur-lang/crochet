Easy Crochet Beanie: Beginner Ribbed Hat Pattern with Sizing Tips

By CozyLoops

Make a cozy ribbed crochet beanie from one rectangle with an easy BLO half-double-crochet repeat, adjustable fit and finishing instructions.

A cozy ribbed crochet beanie is a great next project after a scarf. Instead of increasing in a spiral, this simple design is worked as one rectangle, then seamed into a tube and gathered closed at the top. The stretchy ridges come from crocheting half double crochet through the back loop only (BLO).

You can customize the colors, add a turned-up cuff, or finish with an optional pom-pom. The lavender and cream hats in the images are concept photographs; your actual result will depend on the yarn, row count and gauge.

What You Will Learn

This guide focuses on a practical project rather than a gallery of unrelated ideas: materials, gauge, individual steps, how to correct mistakes, and ways to adapt the finish. All pattern instructions use US crochet terms, and the turning-chain rule is given for each project. Abbreviations: sc — single crochet; hdc — half double crochet; dc — double crochet; ch — chain; sl st — slip stitch; inc — increase; dec — decrease; st — stitch; rep — repeat.

Materials, Measurement and Gauge

  • Smooth medium-weight (#4) yarn; roughly 100–180 g for many adult beanie styles, with yarn usage depending on size and stitch density.
  • A hook compatible with the yarn, approximately 5 mm as a starting suggestion.
  • Tape measure, blunt tapestry needle, scissors and stitch markers.
  • Optional extra yarn for a securely sewn pom-pom on a hat intended for an adult.

Measure the head circumference and the desired depth from crown to brim. This design is intentionally adjustable; it does not claim a single stitch count fits all heads. Make a 10-cm gauge swatch in BLO hdc if precise sizing matters. The rectangle's foundation width determines hat height, and its number of rows determines circumference.

Free Ribbed Beanie Rectangle Pattern

Use US half double crochet (hdc). Every chain-1 turning chain does not count as a stitch.

  • Foundation: Ch 33.
  • Row 1: Hdc in the second chain from the hook and every remaining chain. [32 hdc]
  • Row 2: Ch 1, turn; hdc BLO into each of the 32 stitches. [32 hdc]
  • Rows 3 onward: Repeat Row 2, always working 32 hdc BLO.
  • Continue until the rectangle, when gently stretched, fits comfortably around the intended head with the amount of negative ease preferred for your yarn.
  • Fasten off leaving a long tail for the side seam.

The foundation of 32 hdc is only a sample height. Use fewer or more starting chains if you want a shorter/slouchier hat. Check the depth against a hat you already like before repeating dozens of rows.

Seam the Rectangle and Close the Crown

Fold the rectangle with the first and last rows together, keeping the ridges aligned. Use a tapestry needle with the long yarn tail to whip stitch the side seam through corresponding edges without pulling so tightly that it loses stretch. You now have a tube.

At the top edge, weave a second long strand through several evenly spaced stitch edges around the circle. Pull firmly and gradually to draw the opening closed. If the hole is too large, sew across the remaining gap with a few secure stitches. Turn the hat right side out and weave in all ends.

For a cuffed look, fold the lower edge up by a few centimeters and test how it feels on the forehead. If the fabric is too short after the fold, work a taller rectangle next time.

Optional Pom-Pom and Finishing

A pom-pom makes a fun winter look, but it adds weight and can pull at the gathered crown. Attach it through several strands of the closed top and tie on the inside with secure stitching; check regularly for loosening. Do not add detachable pompoms to baby hats or items that may be handled unsupervised by young children.

Wash and lay the hat flat only as the yarn's care instructions allow. Avoid heat-setting unknown yarn blends and don't pull the crown out of shape while wet.

Adjust the Fit Instead of Guessing

Too tight: Work a few more BLO rows to increase the rectangle circumference. Too loose: Make fewer rows or adjust hook/yarn only after a swatch. Too shallow: Start with additional foundation chains. Too long and slouchy: Reduce the foundation width.

Crochet fabric stretches differently across yarns. Measure the actual width and rib recovery before final seaming, especially if you are making hats for someone else.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Edges lean: You may be dropping the first or last stitch. Count 32 on every row.
  • No ribs appear: Work into the back loop only after Row 1, not under both loops.
  • Seam looks lumpy: Use matching yarn and pair stitches without overtightening.
  • Crown is bulky: Gather evenly and distribute small folds around the top.
  • Hat slides off: Reduce total rows slightly after checking stretched fit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this beanie really beginner friendly?

Yes. The fabric needs only chains and BLO half double crochet, plus basic sewing to finish.

How many rows are needed for an adult beanie?

There is no trustworthy universal number. Count rows until the rectangle's circumference fits your head measurement when gently stretched.

Can I leave off the pom-pom?

Of course. The plain gathered top works on its own and is more versatile.

Why does my ribbing look flat?

Check that every hdc after Row 1 is made in the back loop only; inserting under both loops reduces the ridge effect.

Make the Most of This Project

Before starting, read the complete instructions once, put a stitch marker at the beginning of each round or at the edge of your rows, and check the finished dimensions against the intended use.
