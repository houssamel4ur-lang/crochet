Crochet Balaclava Hood: Beginner Pattern

By Houssam El Ouardy · Published November 30, 2026 · Updated November 30, 2026 · 6 min read

A balaclava is the warmest thing you can crochet for winter: a hood, a hat and a scarf in one piece, with only your face showing. This beginner version is worked from the crown down in half double crochet, with a face opening worked in rows and a ribbed neck that sits flat on your shoulders — no shaping tricks, no seams, just rounds, rows and one join.

The way to wear it this winter is simple: one colour, chunky yarn, and a matching sweater in the same yarn so the set reads as an outfit. Wool or a mohair blend is warm enough for a January walk; cotton works for a cool spring morning instead.

Quick Project Overview

What you will learn: Increasing in the round, working a face opening in rows, rejoining in the round, and front-post ribbing. Skill level: Easy. Terminology: US. Finished size: adult fits a 56–58 cm head, about 55 cm around, 34 cm from crown to neck edge. Time: about 4 hours.

Materials and Tools

Chunky weight yarn (#5) in wool or a mohair blend: about 150 g for the adult size, 100 g for teen, 200 g for large; a 6 mm crochet hook; a yarn needle; scissors; a stitch marker.

Stitches and Gauge

Stitches used: chain (ch), slip stitch (sl st), half double crochet (hdc), increase (inc: 2 hdc in one stitch), front-post half double crochet (fphdc: work a hdc around the post of the stitch below, from the front), back-post half double crochet (bphdc: the same, from the back). Gauge: 12 hdc and 9 rows measure 10 cm with a 6 mm hook. Check your gauge before Round 5 — the crown rounds set the size.

Sizes

| Size | Head | Crown stitches | Face opening | Face rows | Yarn |

|---|---|---|---|---|--- |

| Teen | 52–54 cm | 60 | 16 sts | 9 | about 100 g |

| Adult | 56–58 cm | 66 | 18 sts | 10 | about 150 g |

| Large | 60–62 cm | 72 | 18 sts | 11 | about 200 g |

Work the adult size; teen and large change only where noted.

The Pattern

Work in the round without turning unless the pattern says turn, marking the first stitch of each round.

Crown: Round 1: 6 hdc in a magic ring (6). Round 2: increase around (12). Rounds 3–11: increase 6 stitches evenly each round — (hdc, inc) 6 times (18), (2 hdc, inc) 6 times (24), (3 hdc, inc) 6 times (30), (4 hdc, inc) 6 times (36), (5 hdc, inc) 6 times (42), (6 hdc, inc) 6 times (48), (7 hdc, inc) 6 times (54), (8 hdc, inc) 6 times (60), (9 hdc, inc) 6 times (66). Teen stops at Round 10 (60 stitches); large adds Round 12: (10 hdc, inc) 6 times (72).

Straight rounds: Rounds 12–15 (adult): hdc around (66) — 4 rounds. Teen: Rounds 11–14 (60 stitches). Large: Rounds 13–16 (72 stitches). These four rounds take the hood from the crown down to brow level.

Face opening: Turn the work. Row 1: chain 2, turn, hdc across 48 stitches, leaving the front 18 stitches unworked. Rows 2–10: chain 2, turn, hdc across (48). Teen: work 44 stitches for 9 rows, leaving 16 unworked. Large: work 54 stitches for 11 rows, leaving 18 unworked. The opening is about 15 cm wide and 11 cm tall, from brow to chin.

Neck: Join the yarn at the bottom edge of the hood. Round 1: hdc around the full circle — 48 stitches across the back plus 18 stitches across the front bottom edge (66). Rounds 2–7: hdc around (66) — 6 rounds, about 7 cm of neck. Rounds 8–10 (ribbing): (fphdc, bphdc) around (66) — 3 rounds. Fasten off.

Face edging: Join yarn at the top corner of the face opening. Round 1: work single crochet evenly around the opening — 18 stitches across the top, 1 sc in the end of each of the 10 rows down each side, and 18 across the bottom, about 56 sc in all. Round 2: sc around. Fasten off. The edging frames the face and stops the opening stretching.

Matching sweater idea: a simple half double crochet sweater in the same yarn and colour turns the balaclava into half of a set — wear them together on the coldest days, and the balaclava on its own with any coat the rest of the week.

Styling and Care

Pull the hood on first, then your coat, so the ribbed neck sits inside the collar. If the neck rides up to your chin, work one more plain round before the ribbing next time. Hand wash wool or mohair cold and dry flat; a cotton version can go in the machine on cold, gentle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will it fit over glasses? Yes. The face opening is about 15 cm wide and 11 cm tall on the adult size, so glasses sit inside it comfortably. Put the balaclava on first, then your glasses, and the arms pass through the opening without catching. If your frames are very wide, work the large size — the opening is a row taller.

How warm is it? Very — chunky wool around the whole head and neck, with the ribbed neck sealing out drafts, is warmer than a hat and scarf together. It is comfortable on a cold winter walk with a coat. A mohair blend is warmer still; cotton is noticeably cooler, which is why it suits spring rather than deep winter.

Can I make it in cotton for spring? Yes — use the same stitch counts with a chunky cotton. Cotton stretches less than wool, so check your gauge: if it is stiff, go up half a hook size so the crown still measures 55 cm around.

How do I wash it? Follow the yarn label. Wool and mohair: hand wash cold, press out the water in a towel, and dry flat. Cotton: machine wash cold on gentle and dry flat. Never tumble dry wool — the crown felts and shrinks.
