Two Romantic Crochet Tops: Burgundy Peplum Blouse and Pink Lace Crop Top

A fitted crochet top can feel completely different depending on its silhouette. A burgundy peplum blouse creates a romantic shape with a defined waist and flared lower section, while a pink lace crop top keeps the look light with open stitches, short sleeves and a scalloped neckline. This guide explores both designs, with adaptable construction notes and original stitch instructions.

Design note: The supplied pictures are visual references, not technical patterns. They do not show the exact yarn, hook, stitch counts or construction. The two designs below are original pattern interpretations based on the visible features; they are not exact reproductions of the pictured garments. They have not been physically sample-tested. Make a swatch and check fit frequently. US crochet terms are used.

Compare the two silhouettes

| Feature | Burgundy peplum blouse | Pink lace crop top |

|---|---|---|

| Shape | Fitted waist with flared peplum | Short, lightly relaxed body |

| Sleeves | Long, gently gathered or puffed | Short scalloped sleeves |

| Fabric | Textured, relatively dense stitch | Open shell and chain-space lace |

| Best styling | High-waisted jeans or skirt | Layered over a camisole or tank |

Materials and tools

Choose smooth DK cotton, cotton blend or a soft sport-weight yarn with good stitch definition. For the burgundy blouse, a yarn with enough body helps the waist and peplum hold their shape. For the pink lace top, a lighter yarn and a hook that creates flexible chain spaces will keep the fabric airy.

Gather a crochet hook suited to your yarn, tape measure, stitch markers, tapestry needle, scissors and a notebook. Make a separate swatch for each stitch pattern. Wash or block each swatch as you intend to care for the garment, then measure after it is fully dry.

US abbreviations

  • ch: chain
  • sl st: slip stitch
  • sc: single crochet
  • hdc: half double crochet
  • dc: double crochet
  • inc: two stitches in one stitch
  • dec: decrease
  • sp: space
  • sk: skip
  • rep: repeat
  • BLO: back loop only

Design one: burgundy long-sleeve peplum blouse

The peplum silhouette is built in three zones: a fitted upper body, a shaped waist and a flared lower section. The reference also shows long sleeves and a textured fabric. The easiest way to control the fit is to make the body first, try it on, then add the peplum and sleeves.

Take measurements and plan ease

Measure bust, waist, upper arm, wrist, shoulder width and the desired blouse length. For a close-fitting body, choose a finished waist measurement close to your own, allowing enough room to breathe and move. The bust should not pull across the front. If you prefer a looser blouse, add a few centimeters of ease.

Make a gauge swatch in the main body stitch. Calculate the starting stitch count from your waist measurement and the blocked stitches per centimeter. Do not copy a fixed stitch count from a photograph; yarn, hook and tension change the result.

Work the fitted body

A practical construction is to crochet the front and back as separate panels from the lower waist upward, then join the shoulders and sides. Alternatively, an experienced crocheter can work the body in joined rounds, placing markers at the sides.

For a textured but stable fabric, use alternating rows of hdc worked in the back loop:

Foundation: Chain to the measured panel width, plus one turning chain.

Row 1: Hdc in the second chain from the hook and in each chain across. Ch 1, turn.

Row 2: Hdc in the back loop of each stitch across. Ch 1, turn.

Repeat Row 2 until the panel reaches the underarm. Make the second panel to the same dimensions. Join the shoulders, leaving a neckline opening, then seam the sides from the hem upward, stopping at the armhole markers.

If you want a closer waist, use a smaller hook for a short section only after checking a new swatch. Avoid abrupt decreases that create a tight band or distort the fabric.

Create the peplum flare

Join yarn at the waist edge. Work a setup round or row of sc, distributing stitches evenly. To create a gentle flare, increase gradually rather than placing all increases in one round.

For a simple increase round, repeat *work 4 hdc, 2 hdc in the next stitch* around. On the following increase round, spread increases between the previous increase points. Try the blouse on after each increase round. Stop when the peplum has the amount of fullness you like, then work even rounds to the desired length.

A peplum should flare away from the waist without becoming stiff or heavy. If it ripples dramatically, reduce the frequency of increases. If it hangs like a straight tube, add a few evenly distributed increases.

Shape the long sleeves

Measure from the shoulder to the wrist and around the upper arm and wrist. Join yarn at an armhole and work a setup round of sc, distributing stitches evenly. Work the sleeve in joined rounds using the same hdc texture as the body.

For a gently tapered sleeve, decrease one stitch at each underarm side every several rounds, checking the fit as you go. Stop decreasing when the sleeve reaches the wrist measurement you want. Finish with a narrow cuff of sc or hdc in the back loop.

For a softly gathered sleeve head, add a few extra stitches in the first sleeve round and distribute them evenly; do not add so many that the armhole puckers. A more pronounced puff requires a deliberate sleeve-cap design and should be tested on a sample before making both sleeves.

Neckline and finishing

Work a round of sc around the neckline, placing stitches evenly along the shaped edge. Add a second round if you want a firmer finish. A small scallop edge can soften the neckline, but keep it light so it does not pull the shoulders outward.

Weave in all ends, block gently and try the blouse on before trimming the final tails.

Design two: pink lace crop top

The second design has a shorter body, an open fan-like stitch texture, a rounded neckline and short sleeves. Its airy fabric needs a stable edge at the shoulders, hem and armholes. Use a swatch to decide how open you want the lace to be.

Measure the body and choose the crop length

Measure the fullest part of your bust and choose a finished circumference with comfortable ease. Measure from the shoulder to the point where you want the hem. A crop length can vary widely; use your own torso measurement and preferred rise of trousers or skirt as the guide.

Make front and back panels to the same width. A two-panel construction is easier to adjust than trying to shape the neckline and armholes while working the entire top in one piece.

Work the open fan stitch

Foundation: Chain a multiple of 4 plus 2.

Row 1: Sc in the second chain from the hook. *Ch 3, skip 3 chains, sc in the next chain.* Repeat across. Turn.

Row 2: Ch 1, sc in the first sc. *Ch 3, work 5 dc in the next ch-3 space, ch 3, sc in the next sc.* Repeat across, ending with an sc in the final anchor. Turn.

Row 3: Ch 1, sc in the first sc. *Ch 3, sc in the center dc of the next shell, ch 3, sc in the next sc.* Repeat across. Turn.

Repeat Rows 2 and 3 for the open lace fabric. Keep the same number of anchor spaces on every row and check that the edges remain straight.

Crochet the panels and shape the neckline

Work the front and back panels to the crop length you measured. Finish at a row that leaves a stable upper edge. Join the shoulders, leaving a center opening for the neckline. For a rounded scoop, leave a wider opening at the front than at the back. Try the body on before joining the side seams.

Mark equal armhole depths on both sides and seam upward from the hem. Keep the seam flexible so it does not restrict movement.

Add short sleeves and scalloped edging

Join yarn at the armhole and work a setup round of sc. Distribute stitches evenly around the opening and work a few rounds of the lace pattern adapted to joined rounds. For a short sleeve, stop when it reaches your preferred point on the upper arm. Finish with sc or a small shell edging.

For a scalloped neckline, first work a round of sc so the edge is stable. Then repeat *sl st in the next stitch, skip one stitch, 5 dc in the next stitch, skip one stitch*. Adjust the final repeat so the scallops meet neatly. If the edge waves, use fewer stitches in each shell or a smaller hook.

Finish the hem with a simple sc round or repeat the scallop edge for a more decorative finish.

Blocking and care for both garments

Block the peplum blouse and lace crop top separately, following the yarn label. Pin each garment to its measured dimensions without stretching it aggressively. Allow it to dry fully before trying it on. Store lightweight crochet tops folded if hanging causes the shoulders or sleeves to lengthen.

Troubleshooting

The peplum does not flare: Add a few more evenly spaced increases, then work another sample section before continuing.

The waist feels too tight: Recheck your gauge and waist measurement. Remove the tight section and recalculate rather than forcing the fabric to stretch.

The long sleeves twist: Check that the sleeve join is centered under the arm and that the decrease points are aligned.

The lace crop top curls at the edges: Make sure the foundation chain is relaxed and the edge anchors are consistent. A smaller or larger hook may be needed depending on the swatch.

The scallops ripple: There are too many stitches for the edge length. Reduce the shell size or increase the spacing between shells.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make the burgundy blouse in another color?

Yes. Deep plum, forest green, navy and warm brown all suit the peplum shape. Choose a yarn with enough structure to support the flare.

Can I make the pink lace top longer?

Yes. Add more body rows before joining the panels. Recheck the fit and the intended hem position as you work.

Which project uses less yarn?

The short-sleeved crop top will often use less yarn than a long-sleeved peplum blouse, but actual yardage depends on size, yarn weight, stitch density and garment length.

Are these the exact patterns shown in the pictures?

No. The pictures are styled visual references and do not provide technical stitch instructions. These are original pattern interpretations based on their visible silhouettes and details.

Can I wear the lace top without a layer underneath?

The open stitch pattern is intentionally breathable and may be transparent. Choose a suitable camisole or lining if you want more coverage.

Final fitting checklist

Before wearing either top, check shoulder comfort, neckline stability, sleeve movement, waist ease and the security of every seam. Move your arms forward and overhead to test the armholes. Recheck measurements after blocking because crochet fabric can relax.
