Easy Lacy Crochet Summer Tops for Beginners

Two light, lacy summer tops for beginners: a pink V-neck short-sleeve top and a cream square-neck puff-sleeve top, both worked in open, airy stitch patterns for warm days.

The supplied pins are styled visual references, not technical pattern charts. They do not reveal the yarn, hook, stitch counts or construction used to make the pictured garments. The instructions below are an original, adaptable interpretation, not a claim to reproduce the exact photographed stitches. The pattern has not been physically sample-tested, so make and measure a swatch before committing to the full garment. All stitch terms are US crochet terms.

Quick project overview

  • Project: Two beginner-friendly lacy summer tops, a pink V-neck top with short sleeves and a cream square-neck top with puff sleeves.
  • Skill level: Beginner. You only need the chain, single crochet, double crochet, and simple increases and decreases.
  • Construction: Each top is worked in flat front and back panels, joined at the shoulders and sides, with sleeves added last.
  • Fit: Relaxed through the body, with a shaped neckline and a straight hem that sits at the hip.
  • Stitch texture: Open, lacy stitch work with small gaps that let air through, as shown in the close-ups on both pins.
  • Terminology: US
  • Customization: Make the body longer or shorter, change the sleeve length, or swap the pink and cream colours shown in the pins.

Materials and tools

Choose a smooth, light yarn that feels cool against the skin, such as cotton or a cotton blend in a light to medium weight. Smooth yarn shows the open stitch pattern clearly, while a very fluffy or textured yarn would hide the lace. Pick pink or magenta for the V-neck top and cream or ivory for the square-neck top to match the pins, or choose any two colours you like. Buy enough yarn for a short-sleeve top in your size, plus one extra ball in case you need it. Start with a hook in the range the yarn label suggests, then try one size larger if you want a softer drape, and always test your choice with a swatch first. You will also need a yarn needle, sharp scissors, a tape measure, a few stitch markers, and a notebook to track your rows.

Making the tops step by step

Start by making a swatch in your chosen lacy stitch pattern, wash and dry it as you will wash the finished top, and measure the stitches and rows in a 10 cm square. Use those numbers, with your own bust and body length measurements, to work out your starting stitch count and row count. For the V-neck top, work the back panel first as a straight rectangle, then work the front in two halves, decreasing one stitch at the neck edge every second row so the V opens gradually and evenly. For the square-neck top, work the back panel the same way, then work the front in two straight sections with a gap left between them for the neck opening. Join the front and back at the shoulders, then sew or crochet the side seams from the hem up to the armhole, leaving the armhole open. For the short sleeves of the pink top, work a simple rectangle and join it into the armhole, adding a row of edging to keep the edge neat. For the puff sleeves of the cream top, work a wider rectangle, gather the top edge gently before you set it into the armhole, and add a snug cuff row so the puff holds its shape. Finish both tops with a row of single crochet around the neckline and hem, weave in all the ends, and try the top on before you block it.

Fitting and customization

The easiest way to get a good fit is to measure yourself and compare those numbers with your swatch, rather than guessing from a size label. If you want a looser top, add a few stitches to each panel; if you want it closer to the body, take a few away, and keep both panels the same width so the side seams sit straight. Make the V deeper by starting the decreases sooner, or shallower by starting them later, and widen or narrow the square neckline by changing the gap between the two front sections. Change the body length by working more or fewer rows before the hem, and shorten the sleeves to a cap length or lengthen them toward the elbow. If your yarn is heavier than the yarn in the pins, your top will be warmer and less lacy, so try a larger hook and check the drape on your swatch first.

Styling and care

Both tops look best with simple, light outfits. The pink V-neck top pairs well with white jeans, a denim skirt or light linen trousers, while the cream square-neck top looks lovely with a floral skirt or high-waisted shorts. Wear a plain camisole underneath if you want more coverage, since the open stitch work is slightly see-through, and keep jewellery simple, because chunky necklaces can catch on the lace. Wash in cool water with a gentle detergent, press out the water without wringing, and dry flat so the lace keeps its shape. Fold the tops for storage rather than hanging them, because hanging can pull a crocheted garment out of shape.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a complete beginner really make these tops?

Yes. Both tops use only basic stitches, with shaping limited to simple decreases at the neckline and straight side seams. Work slowly, count your stitches at the end of each row, and make the swatch first.

What yarn works best for a lacy summer top?

A smooth cotton or cotton-blend yarn in a light to medium weight works best, because it is cool to wear, holds the open stitch pattern clearly, and does not stretch as much as a very soft yarn. Avoid very fluffy yarns, since they hide the lace.

How do I keep the V-neck and the square neckline neat?

Work the decreases evenly on both sides of the V, mark the neckline edges with stitch markers, and count often so the two halves stay the same. A row of single crochet around the finished neckline will tidy the edge and help it keep its shape.

Can I make the tops longer or change the sleeves?

Yes. Add rows to the body panels before the hem for a longer top, or stop sooner for a cropped length. For the sleeves, work fewer rows for a cap sleeve, or add rows and a cuff for a longer puff sleeve.
