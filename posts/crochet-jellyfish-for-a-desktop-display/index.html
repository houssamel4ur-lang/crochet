Crochet Jellyfish for a Desktop Display

By CozyLoops

Make a jellyfish for a desktop display with a component plan, counted crochet instructions, assembly notes and practical finishing checks.

Make a jellyfish with an open bell and a short cluster of curled arms for an adult display stand.

Pattern status: Original, untested construction draft. Counts and component logic have been checked where possible; dimensions, fabric behavior and final use have not been physically verified. The hero is an AI concept image, not a photograph proving that these exact instructions produce it. Use the written component plan as the construction reference.

Materials and scale

Smooth cotton crochet thread or fine cotton yarn in seafoam, cream; a hook suited to that yarn; a yarn needle; scissors; removable markers; and any separately supplied base mentioned below. For this miniature, crochet thread with a 1.5–2 mm hook is a starting option. This is not a tested yarn-and-hook prescription. A thicker yarn and larger hook produce a larger object. Use fiberfill only for components that explicitly call for filling. No tested yarn quantity is available; keep enough matching yarn for seams and a second sample if the first needs adjustment.

Size and gauge: This is a component-based prototype rather than a universal finished size. Make one representative piece, measure it resting flat, and compare it with the actual object, container or display space. For a rectangular component, estimated width = stitch count ÷ your stitches per centimeter; estimated height = row count ÷ your rows per centimeter. Circular and stuffed parts require direct measurement. Do not copy a nominal dollhouse scale or fit claim from the image.

Parts to make

  • Seafoam bowl: make 1; starting parameter 4.
  • Cream coil: make 5; starting parameter 20.
  • Seafoam disc: make 1; starting parameter 4.

The starting parameter controls the component instructions below; it is not a measurement in centimeters. Make the stated quantity of each component before attaching anything permanently.

Stitch language

US terms are used: ch = chain; sc = single crochet; sl st = slip stitch; hdc = half double crochet; dc = double crochet; inc = 2 sc in one stitch; sc2tog = single crochet two stitches together. US sc is UK double crochet. Work under both top loops unless directed otherwise. A turning ch-1 does not count as a stitch in the flat single-crochet components. A zero-length middle section is simply omitted.

Crochet the components

1. Seafoam bowl — make 1

Round 1: Make an adjustable ring, work 6 sc into it and close the center. Work in a continuous spiral; mark the first stitch of every round. (6 stitches.)

Round 2: Inc in every stitch. (12 stitches.)

Round 3: Repeat [sc in the next 1 stitch, inc in the next stitch] 6 times. (18 stitches.)

Round 4: Repeat [sc in the next 2 stitches, inc in the next stitch] 6 times. (24 stitches.)

Rounds 5–9: Sc in every stitch, with no increases: 24 sc per round. Leave the upper opening unclosed. Slip stitch into the next stitch and fasten off with a sewing tail. The first plain rounds turn the base into a wall; actual wall angle depends on the fabric.

2. Cream coil — make 5

Row 1: Ch 21; work 2 sc in the second chain from the hook and 2 sc in each remaining chain. (40 stitches.)

Fasten off and let the strip curl. Rotate the curl into the direction required by the assembly. If rolling it into a fixed spiral, tack successive turns from the underside; do not rely on the initial curl alone to retain the finished shape.

3. Seafoam disc — make 1

Round 1: Make an adjustable ring, work 6 sc into it and close the center. Work in a continuous spiral; mark the first stitch of every round. (6 stitches.)

Round 2: Inc in every stitch. (12 stitches.)

Round 3: Repeat [sc in the next 1 stitch, inc in the next stitch] 6 times. (18 stitches.)

Round 4: Repeat [sc in the next 2 stitches, inc in the next stitch] 6 times. (24 stitches.)

Slip stitch into the next stitch, fasten off and retain a sewing tail. Let the disc rest flat. If it cups or ruffles strongly, adjust tension or hook and remake the component before assembly; the count sequence is a starting construction, not a promise of flatness in every yarn.

Arrange and assemble the project

Sew the five coil foundations to the center of the disc. Attach the disc inside the bowl's opening, leaving the coils hanging beneath the bell. Support the bell on a display stand supplied separately rather than adding an untested hanging fixture.

Baste the arrangement with removable thread before final sewing. Use the actual component edges as your guide, and spread attachments over more than one stitch where parts will be handled. Keep knots and tail ends away from the visible or contact surface. Remove the basting only after the permanent join holds the intended position.

The checkpoint that matters here

Spread the arm attachment points across several stitches so the underside does not become one hard knot.

If the parts do not fit together as intended, change the relevant component and recheck it before adding decoration. Do not force a large mismatch by gathering an edge unless the assembly explicitly calls for gathering. Record the yarn, hook, actual component dimensions and any changed rows so the next version is reproducible.

Finishing and care

Weave each tail through secure stitches, reverse direction where practical, and inspect the join before trimming. Care for the textile according to the yarn label and the separately supplied base or hardware. Remove paper, photographs, electronics and other incompatible inserts before cleaning when the construction allows it.
