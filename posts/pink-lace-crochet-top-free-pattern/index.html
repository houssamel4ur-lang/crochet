Pink Lace Crochet Top Pattern: A Pretty Shell Stitch Crop Top

A soft pink lace top is all about the details: an airy openwork fabric, a gently shaped neckline and sleeves finished with a scalloped edge. This original design tutorial translates the look of the reference pin into a wearable crochet project, with a simple two-panel body and separately worked sleeve and neckline finishes.

Quick project overview

  • Project: Short-sleeved lace crochet crop top
  • Skill level: Confident beginner to intermediate
  • Construction: Two matching body panels, joined at the shoulders and sides; sleeves and edging are added afterward
  • Fit: Relaxed, lightly cropped fit
  • Stitch texture: Open mesh with offset shell/fan rows
  • Terminology: US
  • Customization: Measure the panels against your own bust and preferred top length rather than relying on a fixed stitch count

The open lace needs room to drape. Choose a smooth cotton or cotton-blend yarn so the shell details remain visible and the top feels comfortable against the skin.

Materials and tools

  • Lightweight DK cotton or cotton-blend yarn; approximately 500–850 g depending on size, yarn meterage and sleeve length
  • 3.5–4 mm crochet hook, or the size that gives a flexible but stable swatch
  • Matching yarn for sewing seams
  • Tapestry needle
  • Stitch markers
  • Flexible tape measure
  • Scissors
  • Optional: a few removable markers to check neckline and sleeve placement while trying on

Choose a yarn with good stitch definition. Very fuzzy yarn can hide the open chain spaces, while a heavy yarn may pull the lace out of shape. Check the yarn label for care instructions before washing or blocking.

Abbreviations and stitch notes

  • ch: chain
  • sc: single crochet
  • dc: double crochet
  • sl st: slip stitch
  • sk: skip
  • sp: space
  • rep: repeat
  • RS / WS: right side / wrong side

A shell in this tutorial means 5 dc worked into the same chain space. The turning chain does not count as a stitch unless the row says otherwise. Count the sc anchor stitches at the end of each row; they keep the mesh aligned.

Measure first: choosing your size

Measure around the fullest part of your bust while wearing the undergarment you expect to wear with the top. For a relaxed lace fit, aim for a finished garment circumference about 5–10 cm (2–4 in) larger than your body measurement. If you prefer a closer fit, use less ease, but remember that open lace can stretch with wear.

These are starting targets, not guaranteed finished measurements. Divide the target circumference by two to get the approximate width of each body panel. Measure the panel as you crochet and adjust the number of repeats to suit your own gauge.

For the crop length, measure from the shoulder to the point where you want the hem to sit. A common short-top range is approximately 36–43 cm (14–17 in), but choose the length that feels comfortable with your wardrobe.

Make and measure a lace swatch

Work a swatch at least 15 × 15 cm (6 × 6 in) using the mesh and shell rows below. Wash or block it in the same way you plan to care for the finished top, then let it dry completely. Measure the width across several full shell repeats and note how much the fabric relaxes.

If the swatch feels stiff, try a larger hook. If the chain spaces are so loose that the fabric loses its shape, try a smaller hook. Do not use the size table as a substitute for this check: yarn brands and individual tension vary.

The open shell lace stitch

Foundation: Make a chain in a multiple of 4, plus 1 extra chain for the turning edge.

Row 1 (RS): Sc in the second ch from the hook. *Ch 3, sk the next 3 ch, sc in the next ch.* Rep from * across. Turn. You should have a line of sc anchors separated by ch-3 spaces.

Row 2 (WS): Ch 1. Sc in the first sc. *Ch 3, work 5 dc in the next ch-3 sp, ch 3, sc in the next sc.* Rep from * across, ending with an sc in the final anchor. Turn. If your swatch has one extra edge space, adjust the final repeat so both edges remain straight.

Row 3 (RS): Ch 1, sc in the first sc. *Ch 3, sc in the center dc of the next shell, ch 3, sc in the next sc.* Rep across. Turn.

Row 4: Repeat Row 2, placing each 5-dc shell into the ch-3 space between the sc anchors. Continue alternating the shell row and the open mesh row. Keep the same edge treatment on every row.

The pattern creates an airy fan texture. If your row edges do not line up, stop and compare the swatch with the written repeat before continuing; the number of chain spaces and anchor stitches must stay consistent.

Crochet the front and back panels

Make two matching rectangles. Each panel should measure approximately half of your target finished circumference in width. Use the same number of repeats and rows for both pieces.

1. Make a foundation chain using the repeat established by your swatch. Keep the chain relaxed; a tight foundation edge can pull the hem inward. 2. Work Rows 1–4 of the open shell lace stitch. 3. Repeat Rows 2 and 3 until the panel reaches your chosen crop length, measuring from the foundation edge. 4. Finish after a mesh row if possible, so the upper edge is easier to stabilize. 5. Fasten off and weave in the tail without tightening the last row.

Lay the two panels together and compare their width and length. Block them gently to matching measurements before joining. Do not stretch the lace aggressively; it should relax naturally.

Shape the neckline and shoulders

Place the panels with the right sides facing out. At the top edge, mark the shoulder sections and the center opening. The exact opening depends on your preferred neckline and the width of your shoulders.

For a softly rounded scoop neckline, leave a wider center opening on the front panel than on the back. Keep enough fabric at each outer edge to form stable shoulder joins. Pin the panels on a dress form or over a well-fitting top and check that the neckline sits comfortably without pulling toward the neck.

Join each shoulder with a flexible slip-stitch seam or a yarn-needle mattress seam, catching matching edge stitches on both panels. Work loosely enough for the fabric to stretch when worn, but do not leave gaps. Try the body on before closing the sides.

Join the sides and leave armholes

With the panels aligned, mark the bottom of each armhole on both sides. Starting at the hem, seam upward with a yarn needle, stopping at the armhole marker. Repeat on the other side. The unseamed upper sections become the armholes.

Try the top on before adding sleeves. If the armhole feels too tight, unpick a few stitches and lower the seam. If it feels too deep, raise the side seam slightly. Check that both sides match.

Add the short lace sleeves

Join yarn at the underarm. Work evenly around the armhole opening, placing sc stitches along the vertical panel edges and into the shoulder seam. The number of stitches is less important than keeping the opening flat: distribute them evenly and avoid bunching.

Work one setup round of sc, join with a sl st, then work the open shell lace stitch in joined rounds. To adapt the repeat around the sleeve, use a multiple of 4 stitches; add or reduce a few sc in the setup round so the count fits the repeat without pulling.

For a short cap sleeve, work only a few rounds and try it on. For a slightly longer sleeve, continue until the sleeve reaches your preferred point on the upper arm. Finish with a final shell round or a neat sc edging. Repeat for the second sleeve and compare lengths.

Finish the neckline and hem

For the neckline, join yarn at the back shoulder and work a round of sc evenly around the opening. Place one stitch in each suitable edge stitch and distribute stitches evenly around curved sections. Do not crowd the neckline: too many stitches make it ruffle, while too few make it draw inward.

For a delicate scallop, work *sl st in the next stitch, sk 1 stitch, 5 dc in the next stitch, sk 1 stitch* around, adjusting the final repeat neatly at the back. If the edge flares, use a smaller hook or fewer stitches in each shell. If it cups inward, loosen the edging or add a little spacing.

Finish the lower hem with one round of sc or a matching scallop edge. Weave in all tails securely on the wrong side.

Blocking, fit and care

Pin the finished top to the measurements you want, mist lightly if the yarn label permits, and allow it to dry flat. Avoid hanging a wet lace garment: the weight of the water can lengthen the fabric. Recheck the bust, shoulder width, armholes and crop length after it dries.

Wash according to the yarn label. A cotton lace top often benefits from gentle handling and flat drying. Store it folded rather than on a hanger if the fabric stretches easily.

Troubleshooting

  • The mesh pulls inward: Your foundation chain or edge stitches may be too tight. Try a larger hook for the foundation or work the edge more loosely.
  • The shells look crowded: Check that each shell is worked into a chain space, not into an sc anchor.
  • The fabric is too see-through: Use a slightly thicker yarn, a smaller hook, or wear a suitable layer underneath.
  • The neckline waves: There may be too many edging stitches. Remove the edging and redistribute fewer stitches evenly.
  • The sleeve twists: Check that the underarm join is centered and that the setup round is evenly distributed.
  • The front and back do not match: Compare both panels after blocking and adjust the shorter panel before sewing.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the exact pattern from the pin? No. The pin is a visual reference and does not include a stitch chart or construction details. This is an original pattern interpretation designed to capture the pink lace, shell texture and scalloped finish.

Can I make it in another color? Yes. Blush, dusty rose, cream, sage or soft lilac all work well. Choose a yarn with clear stitch definition so the lace remains visible.

Can I make the top longer? Yes. Continue the body panels for additional rows before joining them. Measure against your body as you work.

Do I need to line the top? The open lace is intentionally airy. If you want more coverage, wear a camisole or add a lightweight lining after checking that it does not restrict the fabric.

Can a beginner make it? A confident beginner who can chain, single crochet, double crochet and keep a repeat count can work the panels. Joining, neckline shaping and sleeve placement require careful measuring.

Final fit checklist

Before wearing the top, check that both shoulder seams are secure, the neckline lies flat, the side seams are even, the sleeves match, and all yarn ends are woven in. Try it on and move your arms forward and overhead to make sure the armholes and shoulders remain comfortable.
