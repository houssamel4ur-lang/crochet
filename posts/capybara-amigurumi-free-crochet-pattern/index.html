Capybara Crochet Pattern: Cute Free Amigurumi with Mini Orange

By CozyLoops

Make a soft capybara amigurumi with a round-by-round free pattern, embroidered face, sewn ears and feet, plus an optional little orange.

Capybaras have become an irresistible amigurumi subject thanks to their sleepy expressions, compact bodies and rounded snouts. This original capybara crochet pattern uses a simple rounded body, tiny ears, a contrast-color muzzle and four little feet; there is also an optional mini orange accessory for a playful photograph.

A capybara is a real South American semiaquatic rodent, not a bear or a guinea pig. Its small ears, blunt muzzle and barrel-shaped body make a great starting silhouette for a crochet character.

What You Will Learn

This guide focuses on a practical project rather than a gallery of unrelated ideas: materials, gauge, individual steps, how to correct mistakes, and ways to adapt the finish. All pattern instructions use US crochet terms, and the turning-chain rule is given for each project. Abbreviations: sc — single crochet; hdc — half double crochet; dc — double crochet; ch — chain; sl st — slip stitch; inc — increase; dec — decrease; st — stitch; rep — repeat.

Materials and Pattern Overview

  • Smooth medium brown DK cotton or acrylic yarn; a little dark brown or beige for the muzzle and feet.
  • A 2.5–3 mm crochet hook, or the smallest comfortable hook that makes dense fabric without visible stuffing gaps.
  • Polyester stuffing, stitch marker, tapestry needle and embroidery thread for eyes and nose.
  • For the optional orange: orange and green yarn scraps.

Skills: magic ring, single crochet, increases, decreases, sewing and embroidery. Estimated finished size: approximately 9–14 cm depending on yarn and gauge—not a verified measurement. Work in continuous rounds, not joined rounds.

Crochet the Capybara Body and Head as One Piece

  • R1: 6 sc in a magic ring. [6]
  • R2: inc x6. [12]
  • R3: (1 sc, inc) x6. [18]
  • R4: (2 sc, inc) x6. [24]
  • R5: (3 sc, inc) x6. [30]
  • R6: (4 sc, inc) x6. [36]
  • R7–R16: sc around in each round. [36]
  • R17: (4 sc, dec) x6. [30]
  • R18: (3 sc, dec) x6. [24]
  • R19: (2 sc, dec) x6. [18]
  • Begin stuffing in small loose pieces; stop before stitches spread.
  • R20: (1 sc, dec) x6. [12]
  • R21: dec x6. [6]
  • Cut the yarn with a tail, run it through the front loops of the final six stitches and pull closed. Hide the end inside.

The finished shape is deliberately uncomplicated—a slightly elongated stuffed oval. Give the body a gentle squeeze to flatten its underside.

Small Ears, Muzzle and Four Feet

Muzzle: With darker brown yarn, work R1: 6 sc in MR [6], R2: inc x6 [12], R3: sc around [12]. Fasten off with a long tail, flatten the open side, and sew the muzzle to the front slightly below the midpoint. An oval-looking nose can be suggested with dark embroidery placed across the flattened muzzle.

Ears (make 2): R1: 5 sc in MR [5]; R2: sc around [5]. Flatten gently, leave a tail, and stitch them high on either side of the body. Keep the ears small.

Feet (make 4): R1: 6 sc in MR [6]; R2: sc around [6]. Flatten the openings and sew two feet near the front underside and two near the back. Position all four before sewing, so the capybara can sit without rocking.

Face: Embroider two short closed eyes symmetrically above the muzzle using dark thread. Use simple stitched lines and bury the knots inside. Avoid glued or detachable plastic eyes if the item might be handled by a child.

Optional Mini Orange for Photos

In orange yarn: R1 6 sc in MR [6]; R2 inc x6 [12]; R3–R4 sc around [12]; R5 dec x6 [6]. Add a tiny amount of fiberfill before closing. For a small leaf, chain 4 with green yarn and slip stitch back through the remaining 3 chains; sew it firmly to the top.

Keep the orange as a separate removable photo prop if the finished amigurumi will be displayed, and keep small accessories away from infants. Do not describe it as a certified child-safe toy.

Assembly Order and Troubleshooting

1. Stuff and close the main body.

2. Pin the muzzle to the front and place both ears at equal height.

3. Embroider the face while you can still orient the muzzle.

4. Test the placement of the four feet on a level table before stitching firmly.

5. Weave all yarn tails inward; check each attached part by gently pulling.

If it looks like a bear: Move the ears smaller and closer to the head, enlarge the blunt muzzle slightly, and keep the body barrel-shaped. If it tips over: Move the feet outward a little and add less stuffing near the lower front. If stuffing shows: Use a slightly smaller compatible hook or practice a more even tension.

Styling and Finishing

For a natural capybara palette, try hazelnut, warm tan, caramel and espresso brown. Embroidered eyes look especially calm and match the animal's laid-back character. A linen background and soft window light make stitch definition easier to see in photographs. Frequently Asked Questions

Is capybara amigurumi suitable for beginners?

Yes for beginners who already know sc, increases and decreases. Small parts and assembly add some intermediate-level care.

Can I make the capybara bigger?

Use thicker yarn and a matching hook, then make a gauge sample. Changing yarn affects proportions and may require repositioning the ears and feet.

How much stuffing should I use?

Enough to hold the rounded shape without stretching stitches or exposing fiberfill. Add small portions rather than packing in a single lump.

Is this pattern tested on a physical sample?

No; the stitch counts are editorially checked, while the final appearance and finished measurements still need a real crochet test.
