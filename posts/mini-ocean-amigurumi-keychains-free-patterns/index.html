Turn a few scraps of cotton yarn into a miniature underwater world you can actually take with you. These mini ocean amigurumi keychains include a mint octopus, a little blue whale, a peach starfish and a lilac jellyfish. Each project comes with original written crochet instructions, round-by-round counts, finishing notes and advice for adding a strong key ring.

Unlike a general amigurumi collection, this guide focuses on small hanging charms for keys, backpacks, handbags and zipper pulls. We will also explain when a design is better suited to a bag than a set of frequently used keys.

Pattern note: All stitches use US crochet terminology. The stitch totals have been checked editorially, but these original designs have not been independently crocheted and wear-tested. Actual measurements and durability depend on yarn, tension, construction and use. Make a sample before producing items for sale.

What Makes a Good Crochet Keychain?

A useful crochet keychain is different from a shelf ornament. It should be compact, fairly firm, securely sewn and easy to recognize at a glance. The hanging point must withstand being lifted, pulled and bumped against keys without stretching or unraveling.

For everyday keys, choose shorter, flatter shapes without delicate dangling parts. A two-panel starfish is a particularly good starting design. An octopus or jellyfish looks adorable on a canvas bag, but its curly tentacles may snag inside a pocket. A tiny whale works well on a zipper pull or backpack.

We focus on four beginner-friendly designs. The key ring is a separate component, not part of the crochet stitch pattern; attach it only after the toy is fully finished and its seams are inspected.

Project Overview

  • Mini starfish: Flat-to-lightly-padded; easy to display; best starting option for a keychain.
  • Mini octopus: Rounded body with short curly arms; charming on bags.
  • Mini whale: Oval body with little side fins and tail; tidy silhouette.
  • Mini jellyfish: Dome with short tendrils; better on bags or decorative displays.
  • Difficulty: Beginner with basic amigurumi skills; assembly takes practice.
  • Estimated size: Often around 4–7 cm / 1.5–2.75 in using DK cotton, but this is an estimate, not a tested measurement.
  • Time: Allow approximately 45–120 minutes per charm as a rough planning range; beginners may need longer.

Materials and Tools

Choose smooth DK or sport-weight cotton for defined stitches and reasonable durability. If you want a smaller charm, experiment with finer yarn and a hook suited to it; changing yarn changes the finished size. Cotton blends can also work. Avoid highly fuzzy yarn that hides stitch placement.

Prepare a 2–3 mm crochet hook as a starting suggestion (adjust for yarn and tightness), a stitch marker, sharp scissors, a blunt tapestry needle, small amounts of polyester fiberfill, embroidery thread for faces, and sturdy split rings or keychain hardware. Make sure the ring edges are smooth and the hardware is suitable for the intended use.

Safety: These are decorative accessories, not toys for babies or toddlers. Metal rings, tiny eyes, stuffing and loose parts can pose hazards. Embroider the eyes rather than adding detachable plastic safety eyes, particularly if an item might be handled by a child. Do not give the finished charm to an infant.

Essential Abbreviations (US Terms)

  • MR — magic ring
  • ch — chain
  • sc — single crochet (UK double crochet)
  • sl st — slip stitch
  • inc — increase: make 2 sc in the same stitch
  • dec — decrease: combine 2 stitches into 1, preferably with an invisible decrease
  • BLO — back loop only
  • ( ... ) x6 — repeat the instructions six times
  • [number] — total stitch count at the end of a round

These definitions follow the Craft Yarn Council's US crochet abbreviation standard. Unless specified otherwise, work the small stuffed pieces in continuous spirals, not joined rounds, and move your stitch marker at the start of each round.

Before You Begin: Make a Magic Ring

Wrap the yarn into an adjustable loop, pull up a loop with your hook, make a securing chain, and work the specified number of single crochet stitches around the adjustable loop. Pull the yarn tail carefully until the center closes. For beginners who find this difficult, chain 2 and make the starting stitches into the second chain from the hook; the center may be slightly less neat.

Check your count after every round. If you start with 6 stitches, an increase in each stitch produces 12; six evenly spaced increases on the next round produce 18. This repeatable shaping builds the heads and bodies in the patterns below.

Free Pattern 1: Mini Octopus Crochet Keychain

Best for: Backpack or handbag charm. Yarn: Seafoam green, with a little dark embroidery thread. Construction: One stuffed head plus eight separate short curly arms.

Octopus head

  • R1: 6 sc in MR. [6]
  • R2: inc x6. [12]
  • R3: (1 sc, inc) x6. [18]
  • R4: (2 sc, inc) x6. [24]
  • R5: (3 sc, inc) x6. [30]
  • R6–R9: sc around each round. [30]
  • R10: (3 sc, dec) x6. [24]
  • R11: (2 sc, dec) x6. [18]
  • R12: (1 sc, dec) x6. [12]
  • Add a little fiberfill. R13: dec x6. [6]
  • Cut yarn leaving a tail; weave it through the front loops of the six remaining stitches, draw closed and hide the end.

Embroider the eyes and smile before the opening becomes too narrow, or carefully stitch through the closed body with secured hidden knots.

Make eight little curly arms

For each arm, chain 9. Make 2 sc in the second chain from the hook and in every remaining chain: you will work into eight chains and create 16 sc. The extra stitches make the strip curl. Fasten off with a long sewing tail; repeat until you have eight arms.

Arrange the arms evenly around the lower side of the head, near the final decrease rounds, and sew each one through several body stitches. Spread their attachment points rather than anchoring all eight through the same tiny hole. Pull lightly on each arm to check for loose joins.

Keychain tip: Secure the hanging loop at the top center of the head, opposite the arms. Shorten the arms if the charm will travel inside a pocket.

Free Pattern 2: Mini Whale Crochet Keychain

Best for: A compact bag charm or zipper pull. Yarn: Light blue for the body, optional white for belly embroidery. Construction: A round body, two side fins and two tail lobes.

Whale body

  • R1: 6 sc in MR. [6]
  • R2: inc x6. [12]
  • R3: (1 sc, inc) x6. [18]
  • R4: (2 sc, inc) x6. [24]
  • R5: (3 sc, inc) x6. [30]
  • R6–R10: sc around. [30]
  • R11: (3 sc, dec) x6. [24]
  • R12: (2 sc, dec) x6. [18]
  • R13: (1 sc, dec) x6. [12]
  • Stuff gently. R14: dec x6. [6]
  • Fasten off, draw the remaining opening closed and weave in the tail.

Fins and tail

Make two side fins: 6 sc in MR, then sc around for one more round [6]. Flatten each little piece without stuffing; sew the open edge to each side of the whale.

Make two tail lobes in the same way (6 sc in MR, then 6 sc for a second round). Flatten and sew both lobes next to each other at the back of the body. Angle their tips slightly outward to form a recognizable split whale tail.

Add short embroidered eyes on the front. Optional white stitches or a small white embroidered patch on the lower front can suggest a belly without requiring complex color changes. Sew the ring's hanging loop to the upper body, keeping the fins and tail clear.

Whale troubleshooting: If your tail looks like a single round knob, move the two lobes slightly apart before securing the final stitches.

Free Pattern 3: Mini Starfish Crochet Keychain

Best for: The first everyday keychain in this collection. The starfish uses two matching flat panels with five short points; it can be left almost flat or padded very lightly.

Make the center of the first panel

  • R1: 10 sc in MR. [10]
  • R2: inc in each stitch around. [20]
  • R3: (1 sc, inc) x10. [30]
  • Fasten off. Place a stitch marker after every group of six stitches in R3. You now have five equal groups for the five arms.

Shape each of the five star points

Join yarn at the first stitch of one six-stitch group. Work back and forth; the ch 1 turning chains do not count as stitches.

  • Point row 1: sc in all 6 stitches of the marked group, ch 1, turn. [6]
  • Point row 2: dec, 2 sc, dec, ch 1, turn. [4]
  • Point row 3: dec x2, ch 1, turn. [2]
  • Point row 4: dec. [1]
  • Fasten off and hide the end.

Repeat with fresh yarn at each of the four remaining six-stitch groups. Make an identical second star panel. You should now have two five-pointed stars; re-count the point bases if one point is noticeably wider.

Embroider a tiny face on the front panel and secure the thread at the back. Place both panels together with their wrong sides facing each other; whip stitch around the perimeter through matching edge loops. Add a tiny amount of fiberfill if desired and complete the seam. Keep each point neatly aligned rather than stuffing it until it distorts.

For a flatter keychain, skip stuffing and stitch the two panels firmly together. Attach the hanging loop at the tip of one point, reinforcing that tip so the ring does not pull open the seam.

Starfish troubleshooting: Uneven arms usually mean you worked across more or fewer than six stitches on one point, or your turning chain was mistakenly counted as a stitch.

Free Pattern 4: Mini Jellyfish Crochet Keychain

Best for: Hanging on a tote bag or backpack where the tentacles have room. For frequently used keys, keep the tentacles unusually short to reduce snagging.

Jellyfish dome

  • R1: 6 sc in MR. [6]
  • R2: inc x6. [12]
  • R3: (1 sc, inc) x6. [18]
  • R4: (2 sc, inc) x6. [24]
  • R5–R8: sc around. [24]
  • R9: (2 sc, dec) x6. [18]
  • R10: (1 sc, dec) x6. [12]
  • Stuff lightly. R11: dec x6. [6]
  • Draw the closing yarn through all remaining front loops, pull the bottom closed and hide the tail.

For the tendrils, make six short chains of 7, then work 2 sc into each of the 6 chains beginning in the second chain from the hook. Each strip ends with 12 sc and curls into a short tentacle. Sew their tops evenly around the lower underside of the dome, anchoring each through several stitches.

Embroider small eyes on the side of the dome. Attach a reinforced hanging loop through several stitches near the top center. Check that the jellyfish hangs vertically before finalizing the loop.

Jellyfish troubleshooting: If the dome becomes too tall or narrow, check that R5–R8 each contain exactly 24 stitches and that you have not introduced an extra increase.

How to Attach a Metal Ring to a Crochet Keychain

A keychain is only as practical as its attachment. Do not simply put a split ring through one exposed crochet stitch: the metal can pull or abrade that stitch during repeated use.

Suggested reinforced yarn-loop method:

1. Cut a length of the same durable yarn and thread a tapestry needle with it.

2. Sew through several adjacent stitches at the intended top attachment point, passing through the dense fabric—not just a single outer loop.

3. Make a short folded yarn loop tall enough to accommodate the split ring; avoid an excessively long loop that catches on objects.

4. Pass the working ends through the fabric again several times and knot securely on the inside where possible, then bury tails deeply. If the body is already closed, use concealed traveling stitches and check that the finishing tails cannot easily work loose.

5. Slide the split ring onto the finished loop and test it by gentle hand pulling in several directions while inspecting for stretch or exposed loose stitches.

The loop is a craft attachment method, not a certified load-bearing joint. No crochet pattern can guarantee durability under heavy keys, harsh pulling or repeated abrasion. Replace the charm if the yarn loop becomes frayed.

If using a metal jump ring between the yarn loop and the split ring, choose smooth hardware and close it properly using suitable jewelry pliers. Never rely on glue alone for a keychain that will be handled regularly.

How to Keep Your Mini Ocean Keychains Durable

  • Choose smooth, tightly worked yarn. A smaller compatible hook can reduce visible gaps, but do not make the fabric painfully stiff.
  • Avoid bulky stuffing. Dense little bodies look better and are less likely to reveal fiberfill.
  • Embroider expressions. A small decorative face can be integrated into the fabric instead of relying on glued or detachable pieces.
  • Reinforce sewn-on parts. Give every fin and arm several secure anchoring stitches, especially parts nearest keys.
  • Keep long tentacles for bags. They are more likely to catch on objects when used directly beside a bunch of keys.
  • Check hardware regularly. Look for separated split rings, sharp edges, stretching stitches and frayed hanging loops.
  • Clean gently. Follow your yarn label, avoid prolonged soaking if metal components could rust, and air-dry completely.

Common Beginner Mistakes

The head has tiny gaps

Try a slightly smaller hook suited to your yarn or adjust your tension gradually. Do not pull so tightly that the hook cannot move freely. Insert the needle into both top loops unless a pattern specifically says otherwise.

My stitch counts are wrong

Mark the first stitch at every round, count after each round and never count the marker itself. In these patterns, an increase adds one extra stitch and a decrease removes one stitch.

The metal ring pulls sideways

Move your hanging-loop attachment closer to the true center of gravity. For asymmetrical whale tails or starfish points, try positioning the loop before burying the final yarn ends.

The keychain is bigger than I expected

Finished size varies with yarn, hook and tension. Test the smallest design using thinner cotton before attempting an entire set. Do not assume the AI images are an exact scale reference.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which mini ocean amigurumi is easiest for a beginner?

Start with the starfish if you prefer a flatter shape, or the octopus head if you want to practice increasing and decreasing in the round. The small curl arms add some sewing work.

What yarn is best for mini crochet keychains?

Smooth sport- or DK-weight cotton is a practical starting point because the stitches are visible and the finished fabric generally holds its shape. Your yarn weight and tension control the final dimensions.

How do I make an amigurumi keychain smaller?

Try thinner yarn with a compatible smaller hook while keeping the same stitch-count instructions. Always make a sample: changing the yarn will affect scale and sometimes the stiffness or proportions of sewn pieces.

Can I use safety eyes?

For the smallest charms, embroidery avoids rigid plastic eyes and glue. Small metal rings and other attached pieces still mean these products are not suitable for infants.

Is it safe to use these as baby toys?

No. These instructions are for small decorative accessories, not baby toys, teethers or sleep companions. They can have detachable hardware and small parts. Keep them away from infants and toddlers.

Are these patterns physically tested?

Their round-by-round stitch counts were checked editorially, but the full finished projects and attachment strength were not independently physically tested. Make and inspect a sample before gifting or selling.
